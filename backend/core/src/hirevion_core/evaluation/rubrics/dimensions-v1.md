Score anchors (every dimension, 0 to 10):

- 0: nothing relevant, or the opposite of what was asked.
- 2: attempts it, but mostly wrong, vague or beside the point.
- 5: partly right; the main idea is there, with gaps or errors that matter.
- 8: sound and fairly complete, the answer of a competent practitioner.
- 10: complete and precise, with the depth a senior practitioner would add.

Use the whole range. Do not cluster scores around the middle, and do not give a high score for
confidence or length alone.

The eight dimensions:

- relevance: how directly the answer addresses the question that was asked.
- correctness: how much of what is said is technically right; wrong statements lower it.
- completeness: how much of the expected ground (the expected concepts and the recruiter's
  criteria) the answer covers.
- technical_depth: how far it goes beyond the surface: mechanisms, trade-offs, limits, edge
  cases and real use.
- reasoning: whether the answer explains why, weighs alternatives and reaches its conclusions
  step by step.
- clarity: whether the answer is easy to follow and well organised.
- communication: whether the candidate explains it in a way a listener can use, with precise
  terms and the right level of detail; judged on structure, not accent or grammar.
- evidence: whether claims are backed by concrete examples, experience, numbers or results.
