# ADR-0032: The proctoring engine: the server decides, what it sees itself is the ground truth, and nothing a reviewer dismisses is deleted

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-10-11
- **Sub-project:** SP-14

## Context

- **PRD §54 to §67** describe a pipeline from seven sources to a report, three severities that organisations can configure, a realtime path to a recruiter's dashboard and seven kinds of evidence. **§6.12** of the master plan gives the default severities, says the device only suggests and the server decides (decision D11), and asks for review of events with a reason.
- **`docs/api/contracts/proctoring.md`** (lane B, reviewed by lane C) fixes what the desktop sends. The desktop's monitoring and on-device vision were built against it first and proposed six additions, all decided in the contract's "Changes".
- **ADR-0030** gave interviews one capped Valkey stream per interview and a websocket that replays from an id; the Conductor has a hook for a proctor summary and the CRITICAL-event reminder.
- **SP-12's server** raises `recording.gap` events (`stalled`, `missing`, `corrupt`) and keeps recordings; SP-13 will add the third eye's LiveKit participant.

## Decision

- **The pipeline is one module per stage in `hirevion_core.proctoring`:** normaliser, temporal aggregator, rule engine, evidence manager, then severity, the realtime alert and the report. Rules are pure functions of a policy and the recent history, so every row of the §6.12 table is a unit test.
- **A session pins its policy.** When `POST /proctoring/session` opens the session it writes one self-contained policy (the level's checks, duration tiers, fixed severities, blocking categories, escalation, gaze, nudges, application rules, accommodation effects, the recording flags) from the interview's snapshot, the platform catalogue and the organisation's rules. Later edits never change a running interview, and every event records the policy version it was judged under. SP-22's editor will write `policy.proctoring` overrides into the snapshot, which `build_policy` already merges.
- **The server decides severity.** A device's `severity_hint` is kept as a note. A blocked process's category comes from the rule the session holds at `rule_index`, not from the device's claim. Events outside the level's checks are information; what protects the interview itself (devices, the environment, the lifecycle) always applies. Level `off` stores nothing.
- **Gaze is never critical on its own,** and by default never critical at all: corroboration by another warning is recorded in the event, and a policy may let corroborated sustained gaze become critical. Other warnings that recur (five in ten minutes, by default) become critical. Accommodations change the policy: `relaxed_gaze` keeps gaze at information, `text_answers` the microphone, `third_eye_waiver` the third eye, `assistive_technology` allows screen readers.
- **Idempotency by sequence.** The session records the highest sequence number received and the ranges still missing, not a row per number: a retried number is recognised, a range still missing after 30 s is reported once as a server `TELEMETRY_GAP` (one that fills sooner raises nothing), `acked_through` is the number below the first gap, and `missing` is a flat list. Events that share `event` and `started_at` and carry a `phase` are one episode, updated in place by phase; its severity never goes down.
- **What the server sees is the ground truth.** Channels (camera, microphone, screen, third eye, heartbeat) take votes from the heartbeat, LiveKit's webhooks (track and participant events) and upload continuity (`recording.gap`); a channel is down while any source says so. A channel that stays down for a debounce (5 s) becomes an event, which grows as it lasts and closes when the channel returns; short flaps raise nothing. The device's own report of the same disconnection (within 15 s) joins the server's event. Missed heartbeats (20 s) are a `TELEMETRY_GAP`. A watcher in the workers looks at the sessions that are due: a `scheduler_proctor_sessions_due` function lists them across organisations (`watch_at`), as the interview scheduler does. An interrupted interview pauses the watch; a finished one closes the session.
- **`recording.gap` becomes `SCREEN_CAPTURE_STOPPED` for a screen track and `TELEMETRY_GAP` for the camera and microphone.** `stalled` events are votes that grow with the stall; `missing` and `corrupt` are finished events of the hole's length. A hole joins the event the server already raised for the same track and window, and the parts of it that fall in an excused interruption (SESSION_INTERRUPTED to SESSION_RESUMED) are left out.
- **Evidence is referenced, not copied.** A snapshot is a row from the moment the desktop gets a write-only slot, and is linked once by an event, so evidence can't be replaced afterwards. When a CRITICAL event is raised the server also records snapshot requests (screen, and camera where it matters); they reach the desktop in the heartbeat answer and expire after two minutes. The server adds process metadata, a transcript window and the recording's window as references for events at WARNING and above.
- **Realtime:** alerts are `PROCTOR_EVENT`, `PROCTOR_STATUS` and `PROCTOR_REVIEW` on the interview's existing stream, published after the commit. `/ws/proctoring/{id}` needs `reports.proctoring.view` and `interviews.view`; `/ws/interviews/{id}` hides the proctoring types from those without the first. A dashboard starts from `GET /proctoring/{id}/live` (which carries the stream cursor) and, after a drop, resumes from the last event id it applied.
- **Review:** confirm, false positive or not relevant, always with a reason, insert-only (`proctor_event_reviews`), audited with the previous state, and shown to everyone watching. The event stays in the report. Reviewing needs `interviews.manage` as well as `reports.proctoring.view`; no new permission was added.
- **Viewing is audited:** the report, the live view (once per five minutes per person), the evidence list and the hidden subscribe-only observer token (`POST /proctoring/{id}/observe`).
- **`proctor_events` is not partitioned.** The master plan asks for monthly partitions, but the idempotency key `(session, seq)` and the in-place update of an episode need unique indexes that a partition key would break. A proctored interview holds at most a few thousand events; partitioning can come with retention (SP-22) by moving closed sessions' events.
- **Nudges** are policy: which events, from which severity, a cooldown per kind and a maximum per interview. They go into the interview's hot copy (so text-mode interviews get them from `GET /session`) and onto a stream the interview agent follows to publish `hv.state`'s `notice`. The AI also reminds the candidate of a CRITICAL event at the next turn, through `Conductor.note_proctor_event`, handed over in the event's own transaction as a `proctor.noted` outbox event, so a busy turn lock only delays the note and a refusal is retried.

## Consequences

- **Latency:** an event reaches the dashboard through one commit and one `XADD`; measured through the real HTTP route, DPoP check, database and websocket, the p95 is about 130 ms (`test_proctoring_api.py`).
- **Cross-lane additions:** `MediaControl` gains `observer_token`, and webhook events gain tracks and the third eye; the realtime types gain two; the interview agent follows a nudge stream; `phrasing.proctor_reminder` knows more kinds.
- **The platform catalogue's publisher strings are best effort.** The desktop compares signer names exactly, so a vendor whose certificate says something else is missed until the catalogue is corrected on a real machine. Editing the platform catalogue is SP-22's (`platform.app_catalog.manage`); the app role can only read it.
- **No video tiles yet.** The observer token exists and is audited; the web panel shows statuses, events and the transcript tail, and the camera, screen and third-eye video waits for the media player.
- **Signals are not proof.** Every event is shown with its confidence and source and can be dismissed; the report keeps both what the system saw and what a person made of it.
