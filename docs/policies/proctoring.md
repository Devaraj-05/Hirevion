# Proctoring policy

> **Draft for legal review**, not yet in force. Last updated: 2026-10-09.

Proctoring helps the organisation trust that interviews are fair to every candidate. This policy explains what is checked, what it can and can't do, and how you can respond.

## What is checked

The organisation chooses a proctoring level for each interview (off, basic, standard or strict). Depending on the level, the app and the platform look for:
- **Face:** your face isn't visible, someone else appears, or the camera is covered.
- **Attention:** you look away from the screen for a long time (gaze).
- **Focus:** the interview app loses focus, is minimised or leaves full screen.
- **Displays:** a second monitor is connected, or the display layout changes.
- **Applications:** applications the interview doesn't allow are running (for example remote-control or screen-sharing tools). The app checks names, never contents.
- **Devices:** a phone is visible on camera.
- **Audio:** other voices in the room (strict level).
- **Third eye:** the paired phone camera disconnects or moves (when used).
- **Answers:** answers that read as pasted from an AI tool, or that repeat earlier answers.

The app never records keystrokes, never reads files and never looks at other applications' windows.

## What a signal means

- **No signal decides anything on its own.** Each one is an observation with a time and, where useful, a short evidence clip. It is never proof of cheating.
- **A person reviews** flagged events alongside the recording, and may dismiss them. Every dismissal is logged.
- **During the interview,** the AI may remind you of a rule (for example, "your camera appears to be covered"). A reminder isn't an accusation.

## Fairness

- Lighting, cameras, accents, disabilities and assistive technology can all trigger signals. Reviewers are told this, and signals are shown with their confidence.
- You can ask for accommodations before the interview, such as relaxed gaze checks, typed answers, breaks or extended time. The organisation decides on each request before the interview starts.

## Your rights

- You may ask the organisation which events were recorded about your interview and to explain any decision that relied on them.
- You may ask for a human review of a decision.
- See [support](support.md) for how to raise a concern, and the [retention policy](data-retention.md) for how long proctoring events are kept (90 days by default).
